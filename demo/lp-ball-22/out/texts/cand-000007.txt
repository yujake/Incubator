1.
Introduction
Computing the volume of sections or projections of convex sets is not easy, even in
speciﬁc cases. However, in the last decades several authors managed to produce workable
formulas, often related to probability and to Fourier analysis, and to determine extremal
volumes of sections of certain bodies. The example of the cube Bn
∞= [−1, 1]n was
settled ﬁrst: Hadwiger’s result in [11] implies that the hyperplane sections through the
origin have no less volume than the canonical sections. Vaaler [22] was able to show
this for sections of arbitrary dimension. The largest hyperplane section of the cube was
found by Ball [2], an important result which led to a negative answer to the Busemann–
Petty problem in dimensions larger than 10 (see [3] for results in larger codimension).
