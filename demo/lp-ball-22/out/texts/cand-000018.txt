Abstract
We present proofs of Szarek’s moment comparison and Ball’s cube
slicing inequalities based on elementary convexity and majorisation. The
key ingredient is a moment-lifting argument which allows us to use Schur
monotonicity, avoiding the integral inequalities used in the original Fourier-
analytic approaches.

5.3
A bit of history and wider context
Moment comparison inequalities are most interesting as reversals of the plain

5.4
Future work
Stability.
Once a sharp inequality and its equality cases are known, the next
natural question is whether near equality forces the coefficients to be close to an
extremiser. For Szarek’s inequality, see [18]; for Ball’s inequality, see [15, 49],
with explicit estimates for both in [23]. For higher moments, dimensions and
other aspects, see also [17, 21, 32, 33, 34] The analogous question for polydisc
slicing is treated in [26]. We plan to retain the losses in our lifting and ma-
jorisation arguments to give direct, more transparent proofs of such stability
estimates in the companion paper [66], with better numerical constants.

ting, have been also studied in [16, 20, 34]. Moreover, very recently, stability of
Brzezinski’s bound has been established in [9], albeit without explicit constants.
Our companion paper will rectify that.
Other bodies.
A different stability question concerns perturbations of the
summand distribution; see [21].
In particular, the resilience theorem of [23]
says that the two-coordinate normal continues to maximise sections of real ℓp
balls for all sufficiently large p, uniformly in the dimension, and to minimise
projections of ℓq balls for q sufficiently close to 1. It would be interesting to
find a lifting adapted to these perturbed laws which remains sharp at the two-
19
===== page 20 =====
coordinate sum, and thereby obtain a simpler proof of resilience.
The simplex gives another natural test. Webb’s upper bound [69] already has
a proof based on comparisons of half-line moments of log-concave densities; see
[68, Lemma 1.7]. This fits the bridge philosophy, although it is not the same
replacement identity. The negative-moment point of view is developed further
in [51]. For the opposite, lower bound, Ambrus and G´argy´an [1] recently proved
that the minimal central hyperplane sections are parallel to facets, following the
asymptotically sharp result of Tang [65]. We would like to understand whether
lifting and convexity can give an alternative proof of this lower bound.
